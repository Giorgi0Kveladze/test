## Primary ICP: The Overwhelmed Third-Year CS Student

This ICP describes the person most likely to experience your committed problem most acutely. They are your primary target.

### 1. Segment

A 3rd-year undergraduate Computer Science student at Kutaisi International University (KIU) who is currently taking at least 3 project-based or heavily evaluated courses in the same semester. They are required to check multiple systems daily for course information.

**Test:** Could you walk into KIU CS building and within 10 minutes identify someone matching this description?  
**Answer:** Yes. Ask anyone in the library what year they are and how many project courses they're taking.

---

### 2. Context

It is the middle of the semester (Week 8+). They are sitting in the library or their dorm with multiple IDEs and browser tabs open, trying to prioritize which assignment to start on for the week, while their phone is buzzing with Messenger notifications. They have at least 3 active project deadlines but can't remember which is due first.

**Test:** Is this a specific enough context that you could design an interview question around it?  
**Answer:** Yes. "Tell me about the last time you sat down to work on your assignments for the week. Walk me through it from the moment you decided to start working."

---

### 3. Primary Pain

The sudden panic of realizing an assignment is due tonight at 23:59 (that they didn't see announced), or reading the syllabus after submitting and realizing they missed a specific formatting requirement that was posted exclusively on Teams or in a WhatsApp group.

**Test:** Would someone matching this ICP read this description and say "that is exactly what it feels like"?  
**Answer:** Yes (confirmed through 4 interviews).

---

### 4. Current Workaround

They rely heavily on the "Course Representative" or more organized friends in group chats to compile a list of requirements. They take screenshots of Teams announcements and leave them in their phone notes. They ask peers "did we have anything due?" multiple times per week in class group chats.

---

### 5. Job to Be Done

"When I am juggling multiple ongoing university deliverables, I want to see a unified, prioritized list of all scattered actionable requirements, so I can start my actual coursework without the anxiety of missing something crucial."

---

### 6. Success Signal

- They would stop repeatedly asking peers "did we have something due today?" in group chats
- They would stop opening platforms (Teams, Moodle) solely to check for updates out of fear  
- They would feel confident they haven't missed anything after checking a single place
- They would recover 20-30 minutes per day currently spent on platform-checking and spend it on actual work

---

### 7. Where to Find Them

- The KIU campus library during peak afternoon hours (14:00-18:00)
- KIU dorm study rooms in the evening
- 3rd-year CS course group chats on Messenger/WhatsApp where we can drop a quick interview request survey
- Computer lab during assignment submission windows

---

### 8. Why You, Why Now

We are also 3rd-year KIU CS students facing the exact same problem right now. We have immediate rapport and empathy with them, meaning they will give us honest feedback without feeling judged about their organization. They see us as peers, not outsiders.

**Urgency:** It's currently Week 11 of the semester. The problem is acute right now. Students are 2 weeks from final deadlines and panic is high.

## Secondary ICP: The Group Project Coordinator

This ICP describes a secondary persona worth monitoring. This person experiences a related but distinct problem around task coordination.

### 1. Segment

A KIU student (any major, though frequently CS or Business) who often acts as the unofficial "Team Lead" or coordinator in 3-to-5 person group projects across 2+ courses.

---

### 2. Context

It is 36 hours before a major group project submission deadline. They are trying to assemble the final presentation or codebase and only then realize a team member failed to complete their assigned part because the assignment of that specific role got lost in a 200+ message WhatsApp chat from 2 weeks ago.

---

### 3. Primary Pain

Deep frustration and resentment at having to redo work themselves at the last minute because someone else dropped the ball due to poor task visibility. The emotional weight of feeling like the only one who cares about the project.

---

### 4. Current Workaround

Using the "Reply" feature in Messenger constantly to refer to old tasks. Occasionally trying to force the group to use Trello or a shared Google Doc checklist, though 70% of the time teammates ignore these tools and go back to chat.

---

### 5. Job to Be Done

"When I am collaborating on a graded group assignment, I want an integrated way to persistently attach task ownership to conversations, so I can hold my teammates accountable without feeling like a project manager."
